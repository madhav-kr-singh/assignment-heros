### Read this first

You have been shortlisted. The next step is a practical task for the role you applied to. Every role in this kit has two tasks

- Task A and Task B. Complete both. They are designed to show how you think and work at a professional level.

##### The rules

- Use AI - properly. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and any other model are fully allowed across both tasks. Use them to
    learn the concepts you do not know yet, to speed up the parts you do, and to pressure-test your own thinking. We work
    with these tools every day, so we would rather see how well you direct them than watch you avoid them. Add one short
    paragraph telling us where you used AI and what you changed afterwards.
- Make it unmistakably yours. Many people are working on this exact brief right now. Do not copy another participant,
    and do not hand in whatever a model gives you on the first prompt. Bring your own angle, your own voice, and your
    own judgment - the parts that are distinctly you are exactly what we are looking for, and they are what the interview will
    be built around. A rough submission that is clearly yours beats a polished one that could have come from anyone.
    Copied or purchased work means immediate disqualification, and we do check.
- Timeline: Submit within 3 days of receiving this brief. If you need longer for a genuine reason, tell us before the
    deadline rather than after it.
- Do only your role's tasks. Find your role in the contents list below and complete Task A and Task B for that role only.
- Send links, not files. Put everything in a Google Drive folder set to "anyone with the link can view", named
    Role_YourFullName, and send that single link. Broken or private links count as no submission.
- Walkthroughs are not optional. Where a task asks for a Loom or screen recording, submissions without it are not
    reviewed. Two to three minutes is enough.
- Your work stays yours. Submissions are used to evaluate you. If we ever want to publish or reuse your work, we will
    ask you first and credit you.
- Assumptions are part of the test. Where a brief is ambiguous, make a sensible call and state the assumption in your
    submission. Resourcefulness counts.

Every submission is reviewed by our team, and shortlisted candidates are invited to an interview where you will walk
through your work and the thinking behind it. That is why your own voice in the task matters so much - the conversation
starts from what you made.
We receive a very large volume of applications, so please be patient with us. Reviews take time and we work through
them in order. Our team will reach out to you on Instagram, and you may be asked to fill in a short form before your
interview is scheduled. There is no need to send repeated follow-up messages - as long as you are following the account,
we can reach you.

```
###### TASK A - Build a lead platform, not a lead form

```
Build a lead management application that a small sales team could actually use.
a) Public capture form plus an authenticated application with at least two roles - admin and member - and enforced
permissions on both client and server.
b) Lead lifecycle: status pipeline, assignment to a user, notes with timestamps, and an activity trail.
c) A JSON API for leads with pagination, filtering, and proper status codes - documented in the README.
d) Automated tests covering auth rules and at least two core flows, plus deployment on any free tier.
Deliverables
```
- Public GitHub repo with tests
- Deployed application + credentials for each role
- API documentation
```

```
###### TASK B - Inherit and improve

```
You are handed a working but poorly built codebase with no tests, business logic inside route handlers, direct database
calls from the frontend, and secrets in the repo. It serves real customers and cannot go down.
a) Write the assessment: what you would fix, in what order, and the risk of leaving each issue in place.
b) Produce a migration plan that never requires a big-bang rewrite - name what ships in week 1, month 1, and quarter 1.
c) Demonstrate one refactor concretely: take a realistic bad code sample you write yourself, refactor it, and explain what
improved.
d) Define the engineering standards you would introduce and how you would get a resistant team to adopt them.

```
- Before/after refactor with commentary
- Standards proposal
```
